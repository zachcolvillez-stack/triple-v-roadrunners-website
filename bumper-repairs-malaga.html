Repair or replace your bumper?

This is the question we get asked most, and the answer is more often "repair" than people expect. Modern bumpers are plastic, and plastic can be reshaped, welded and filled in ways sheet metal cannot. A bar that looks badly damaged can frequently be brought back.

DamageUsual answerNotes
Scuffs and scrapes through the paintRepairPrepare and respray; very commonly done
Cracks, with the bar otherwise soundRepairPlastic can be welded and reinforced from behind, then refinished
Dents and deformation, mounts intactRepairPlastic can often be reshaped
Broken mounting tabs or clipsUsually repairTabs can often be rebuilt; if not, replacement
Shattered, torn, or missing sectionsReplaceNot enough sound material left to repair reliably

What is behind the bumper matters. The plastic cover is cosmetic; behind it sit the reinforcement bar, energy absorber and often parking sensors, cameras or radar. Those are checked as part of the quote, because a tidy-looking cover over damaged structure is not a finished repair.

How a bumper repair is done

  • Removal where needed, so the back of the bar can be worked on and the mounts and structure behind inspected.
  • Cracks welded or bonded and reinforced from behind, so the repair holds rather than reopening with vibration.
  • Deformation reshaped and the contour re-established.
  • Surface preparation — sanded back and levelled, using products suited to flexible plastic substrates.
  • Adhesion promoter and primer, because paint does not bond to bare plastic the way it does to primed metal.
  • Colour matched and resprayed, blended into the adjacent panels where the finish requires it.
  • Refitted and aligned so the panel gaps against the guards and bonnet are even.

Bumpers are also a common place for colour mismatch, because plastic bars often weather at a slightly different rate to the metal panels around them. That is a matching problem, and it is dealt with the way any other match is — off the vehicle. See car paint repairs.

How the inspection and quote works

Every job starts the same way: we look at the vehicle in person. Photos are useful for a rough idea over the phone, but panel damage hides things — a dent that looks superficial can have a cracked bracket or a distorted mounting point behind it, and a scuff can turn out to have gone through to bare metal.

  • Bring the vehicle in, or send photos first if you want an initial idea before making the trip.
  • We inspect the damage and check what is behind the visible panel — mounts, brackets, clips, adjacent panels and panel gaps.
  • We work out repair versus replace for each damaged part, and explain why.
  • You get a written quote itemising the panel work, parts, preparation and refinishing.
  • Nothing extra happens without your say-so. If hidden damage turns up once the panel is off, you get a phone call and a revised quote to approve — not a surprise on the final invoice.

On hidden damage: it is common, and any repairer who tells you a collision quote is guaranteed final before dismantling is guessing. We would rather explain that up front than have the conversation later.

How long repairs take

We give you a timeframe with your written quote, because the honest answer depends on the job. What drives it:

  • How many panels are involved, and whether any need replacing rather than repairing.
  • Parts availability — this is often the biggest variable. A common part may be on the shelf locally; something less common can mean waiting for it to come from over east.
  • How much refinishing is needed, including blending into adjacent panels.
  • Curing time, which cannot be shortened without compromising the finish.
  • Whether hidden damage is found once panels come off.

A small localised repair is a much shorter job than a multi-panel collision repair waiting on parts. Rather than quote you an average that will not apply to your car, we will tell you what your specific job needs when we quote it.

Frequently asked questions

Can a damaged bumper be repaired instead of replaced?

In most cases, yes. Plastic bumpers repair well — scuffs, cracks and dents can usually be repaired, reinforced from behind where needed, and resprayed. Replacement is generally only necessary when the bar is shattered, torn, or missing sections.

Is repairing a bumper cheaper than replacing it?

Usually, because you are not paying for a new part. The repair still involves preparation and refinishing, so it is not free — but for typical scuff, crack and dent damage it is normally the better-value option. We will quote both if replacement is a genuine consideration.

Will a repaired crack open up again?

Not if it is done properly. The crack needs to be welded or bonded and reinforced from behind, not just filled from the front. A repair that has only been filled cosmetically is the kind that reopens with vibration and flex.

Do you check the parking sensors and structure behind the bumper?

Yes. The plastic cover is only part of the assembly — the reinforcement bar, energy absorber and any sensors, cameras or radar behind it are inspected as part of the quote.

Why doesn't my bumper match the rest of the car?

Plastic bumpers often weather at a slightly different rate to the surrounding metal panels, so even an original bar can drift out of match over years. Matching is done against the actual paint on the vehicle and blended into the adjacent panels.
