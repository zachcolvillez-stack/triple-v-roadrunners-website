What we repair

Smash repair covers everything from a car park knock to serious collision damage. What comes through the workshop most often:

  • Front and rear end collision damage — bumpers, bars, guards, bonnets, boot lids, lights and the mounting structure behind them.
  • Side impact damage — doors, quarter panels, sills, mirrors and pillars.
  • Panel damage with distorted body lines that needs straightening rather than replacing.
  • Damage where panel gaps and alignment have shifted, so doors, bonnets or boots no longer sit or close correctly.
  • Cosmetic damage — scrapes, scuffs, dents and paint damage that has not affected structure.
  • Hail and storm damage to horizontal panels.

If you are not sure whether something is worth repairing, bring it in. Part of the job is telling you honestly when damage is minor enough to live with, and when leaving it will let rust or water in and cost you more later.

How the inspection and quote works

Every job starts the same way: we look at the vehicle in person. Photos are useful for a rough idea over the phone, but panel damage hides things — a dent that looks superficial can have a cracked bracket or a distorted mounting point behind it, and a scuff can turn out to have gone through to bare metal.

  • Bring the vehicle in, or send photos first if you want an initial idea before making the trip.
  • We inspect the damage and check what is behind the visible panel — mounts, brackets, clips, adjacent panels and panel gaps.
  • We work out repair versus replace for each damaged part, and explain why.
  • You get a written quote itemising the panel work, parts, preparation and refinishing.
  • Nothing extra happens without your say-so. If hidden damage turns up once the panel is off, you get a phone call and a revised quote to approve — not a surprise on the final invoice.

On hidden damage: it is common, and any repairer who tells you a collision quote is guaranteed final before dismantling is guessing. We would rather explain that up front than have the conversation later.

Repair or replace — how we decide

This decision drives most of the cost of a smash repair, so it is worth understanding how it is made. It is not simply about which is cheaper on the day.

SituationUsual approachWhy
Shallow dent, paint intact or lightly damaged, metal not stretchedRepairThe panel can be brought back to shape without compromising it
Sharp crease, stretched metal, or damage on a complex curveOften replaceStretched metal will not return to its original shape reliably; a forced repair can show later as ripple
Split, torn or heavily cracked panelReplaceStructural integrity and corrosion protection cannot be restored properly
Plastic bumper with cracks or scuffs, mounts intactUsually repairPlastic repairs well; see bumper repairs
Damage to mounting points or a panel that carries loadReplaceSafety and fit — a compromised mounting point affects everything bolted to it

We will always tell you which parts of a quote are repairs and which are replacements, and what the reasoning was. If a repair is possible but we would not recommend it, we will say that too.

Colour matching, and why it is the hard part

Getting a repair to disappear is mostly about the paint, not the panel. Factory colours are not one fixed recipe — the same colour code can vary between production batches and between plants, and every car on the road has faded from UV exposure since it left the factory. A Perth car that has spent years parked outside has moved a long way from its original code.

So matching means working from the vehicle in front of us, not just the code on the compliance plate. That involves reading the existing paint, mixing to suit it, and spraying out a test card to check the match before anything goes near the car.

The other half is blending. On a metallic or pearl finish especially, paint is carried out into the adjacent panel and faded so there is no hard edge where new meets old. A repair that is technically the right colour but stops dead at a panel line will still be visible. That blending work is a large part of why a quality refinish takes the time it does.

Preparation and quality control

Paint only ever looks as good as what is underneath it. High-gloss automotive finishes are unforgiving — any imperfection in the surface below shows through, and often shows up worse once the clear coat goes on and starts reflecting light.

  • Surface preparation — stripping back the damaged area, filling and shaping where required, then progressively finer sanding to a flat, even surface.
  • Masking and protection so overspray does not reach glass, trim, seals or adjacent panels.
  • Priming the prepared area so the topcoat has a consistent base to sit on.
  • Colour and clear coat, blended into the adjacent panels where the finish requires it.
  • Curing — paint needs time to harden properly, and rushing it is how a finish gets marked before the customer has even driven away.
  • Final inspection — panel gaps and alignment checked, the finish checked in good light for texture and match, and the vehicle cleaned before handover.

How long repairs take

We give you a timeframe with your written quote, because the honest answer depends on the job. What drives it:

  • How many panels are involved, and whether any need replacing rather than repairing.
  • Parts availability — this is often the biggest variable. A common part may be on the shelf locally; something less common can mean waiting for it to come from over east.
  • How much refinishing is needed, including blending into adjacent panels.
  • Curing time, which cannot be shortened without compromising the finish.
  • Whether hidden damage is found once panels come off.

A small localised repair is a much shorter job than a multi-panel collision repair waiting on parts. Rather than quote you an average that will not apply to your car, we will tell you what your specific job needs when we quote it.

Frequently asked questions

How much do smash repairs cost in Perth?

It depends entirely on which panels are damaged, whether they can be repaired or need replacing, how much refinishing and blending is needed, and part prices for your vehicle. That is why we inspect the car and give you a written itemised quote rather than a phone estimate that will not hold.

Do you provide written quotations?

Yes. Every job is quoted in writing after an inspection, with the panel work, parts, preparation and refinishing itemised so you can see what you are paying for.

What happens if hidden damage is found after dismantling?

We stop, call you, and give you a revised quote to approve before continuing. Hidden damage is common in collision work — brackets, mounts and clips behind a panel are not visible until the panel comes off. You will never find extra work on the invoice that you did not approve.

What information do you need for a repair quote?

Ideally the vehicle itself, plus its make, model and year. If you want an initial idea before coming in, send clear photos of the damage from a few angles including one wider shot showing the whole panel and the panels either side of it.

Can you repair hail damage?

Yes, hail damage to horizontal panels is something we handle. The approach depends on how many dents there are, how deep they are, and whether the paint surface has been broken — bring the vehicle in and we will assess it.
