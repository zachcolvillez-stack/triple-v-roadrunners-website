What panel beating actually is

Panel beating is the process of returning damaged sheet metal to its original shape, using controlled force from both sides of the panel rather than simply pulling the dent out. Done properly, the metal ends up the correct shape and the correct thickness, with its original strength intact.

It is the part of a repair that most determines whether the finished job looks right. Filler and paint can hide a lot, but they cannot hide a panel whose curve is wrong — the reflection along the body line gives it away instantly, particularly in direct sun. A repair that reads as "repaired" from ten feet away almost always failed at this stage, not at the paint stage.

Where the skill is

  • Reading the damage — working out the order the metal moved in, because it has to be brought back in roughly the reverse order.
  • Working the high and low spots together, rather than pushing a dent out and creating a bulge around it.
  • Managing stretched metal. Impact can stretch a panel so there is more surface area than the shape needs — that has to be dealt with, not filled over.
  • Restoring the body line. The creases and curves that catch the light are the least forgiving part of any panel.
  • Keeping filler to a minimum. Filler is for final levelling, not for making up shape. A panel that needs thick filler to look right was not finished properly.
  • Checking panel gaps and alignment so doors, bonnets and boots sit and close the way they should.

Panel beating versus paintless dent repair

Panel beatingPaintless dent repair (PDR)
SuitsDamage where paint is broken, metal is creased or stretched, or the body line has movedShallow dents with the paint completely intact and unbroken
Refinishing neededYes — the repaired area is prepared and repaintedNo, which is the whole appeal
Typical useCollision damage, creased panels, anything with paint damageDoor dings, minor hail, trolley knocks
LimitsRequires colour matching to be rightCannot fix broken paint, sharp creases, or damage on a panel edge

They are different tools for different damage rather than competitors. If your damage genuinely suits PDR — a clean shallow dent with unbroken paint — that is worth knowing, and we will tell you. Where the paint has been broken or the metal creased, panel beating and refinishing is the repair that will actually last.

How the inspection and quote works

Every job starts the same way: we look at the vehicle in person. Photos are useful for a rough idea over the phone, but panel damage hides things — a dent that looks superficial can have a cracked bracket or a distorted mounting point behind it, and a scuff can turn out to have gone through to bare metal.

  • Bring the vehicle in, or send photos first if you want an initial idea before making the trip.
  • We inspect the damage and check what is behind the visible panel — mounts, brackets, clips, adjacent panels and panel gaps.
  • We work out repair versus replace for each damaged part, and explain why.
  • You get a written quote itemising the panel work, parts, preparation and refinishing.
  • Nothing extra happens without your say-so. If hidden damage turns up once the panel is off, you get a phone call and a revised quote to approve — not a surprise on the final invoice.

On hidden damage: it is common, and any repairer who tells you a collision quote is guaranteed final before dismantling is guessing. We would rather explain that up front than have the conversation later.

Preparation and quality control

Paint only ever looks as good as what is underneath it. High-gloss automotive finishes are unforgiving — any imperfection in the surface below shows through, and often shows up worse once the clear coat goes on and starts reflecting light.

  • Surface preparation — stripping back the damaged area, filling and shaping where required, then progressively finer sanding to a flat, even surface.
  • Masking and protection so overspray does not reach glass, trim, seals or adjacent panels.
  • Priming the prepared area so the topcoat has a consistent base to sit on.
  • Colour and clear coat, blended into the adjacent panels where the finish requires it.
  • Curing — paint needs time to harden properly, and rushing it is how a finish gets marked before the customer has even driven away.
  • Final inspection — panel gaps and alignment checked, the finish checked in good light for texture and match, and the vehicle cleaned before handover.

Frequently asked questions

How long does panel beating take?

It depends on how many panels are involved, how badly the metal has moved, and whether body lines and panel gaps need re-establishing. A single straightforward panel is a considerably shorter job than multi-panel collision damage. We give you a timeframe with your written quote.

What is the difference between panel beating and paintless dent repair?

Panel beating reshapes the metal and the repaired area is then prepared and repainted, which is what you need when paint is broken or metal is creased. Paintless dent repair works the dent out from behind without repainting, which only works on shallow dents with completely intact paint.

Will the repair be visible afterwards?

A properly executed repair should not be visible in normal light. Getting there depends on the panel being returned to its correct shape, minimal filler used only for final levelling, and the paint being matched and blended into the adjacent panels.

When should a damaged panel be replaced instead of repaired?

When the metal is stretched or sharply creased, when the panel is split or torn, when damage has affected a mounting point, or when the damage sits on a complex curve that will not return to shape reliably. We assess each panel and tell you which we are repairing and which we are replacing, and why.

Do you use filler?

Only for final levelling, which is what it is designed for. Filler should never be used to create shape that the metalwork should have restored — a panel needing thick filler to look right has not been finished properly, and it tends to show up later.
